Silverman's rule, and how Her breaks it

The female voice always returns to the body.

In popular film

Only temporary

A woman's voice may float free for a while, but she is always brought back to her body. A voice that stayed free would threaten the male gaze.

In Her

Permanent, yet still gendered

Samantha never gets a body. Yet her voice stays feminine and sexualized through:

Motherhood, all through the film

Mothers everywhere.

  • i

    The setup

    While installing, the OS asks Theodore about his relationship with his mother.

  • ii

    Amy's film

    Amy films her sleeping mother to study the unconscious, in contrast with Samantha's growing consciousness, which leads to her liberation.

  • iii

    The subway

    Theodore secretly looks at a nude photo of a pregnant celebrity, hiding it like a naughty child.

  • iv

    The phone call

    A voice-only sex chat with a stranger. It foreshadows the romance with Samantha and reveals his complicated relationship with his mother.

  • v

    Nature vs. nurture

    The "sacredness" of motherhood is shown as culturally constructed.

Acoustic mirror · Silverman 1988

Her voice echoes his weaknesses.

An Oedipal reading: there is no father figure. Theodore is surrounded by women and acts like a "castrated child" seeking guidance.

Even dating an AI is framed as a personal failure after his marriage ended.

Scene A · Figures 3–4

The surrogate.

Samantha invites Isabella (Portia Doubleday, voiced by Soko) to be her body. Isabella stays silent, wearing a tiny camera and an earpiece, while Samantha's voice leads.

Theodore is uncomfortable. The seduction and dancing look grotesque, so the gaze is subverted. The scene fails: Isabella locks herself in the bathroom.

The disconnection, reversed

Seen or heard. Never both.

Isabella has a body but must stay mute: objectified for male pleasure. Samantha has a voice but no body.

When Isabella hides in the bathroom, she becomes heard but not seen, just like Samantha. Two actresses for one woman intensify the split.

The verdict

A subject, not an object.

What the film achieves

A powerful, utopian subject

Samantha is neither an object nor a threatening "other."

What remains

Patriarchal limits

Especially in how the female AI's sexuality is represented.

A mirror of human desire

A one-way mirror.

The AI mirrors the human desire to know and to become. The film is really about the human mind, not computer consciousness.

In "her", Theodore sees his own emotional limits.

Control and fidelity

Not infidelity. A skill.

Her simultaneous relationships with many humans and operating systems are shown as an ability. This reframes the "unfaithful woman" trope.

The parallel: humans' anxiety to control technology resembles men's desire to control women.
